About

I'm a postdoc with Rhiju Das at Stanford. I invented FAST-MaP, a fast way to read RNA structure. I use it to check whether AI-designed RNAs fold as predicted, and I design mRNAs that use structure to control protein output. The results go back to the models, so they can design the medicines of the future.

I came to RNA through cell division, studying chromosome segregation for my master's at JNCASR with Kaustuv Sanyal, and mRNA stability during mitosis for my PhD at the University of British Columbia with Christopher Loewen and Calvin Roskelley.

Research

Preprint

FAST-MaP: chemical mapping of RNA structures without the infrastructure

DNA template to per-nucleotide RNA structure data in under a week, using commercial gene synthesis, Nanopore sequencing and a public analysis server. No sequencing core or custom code needed, and I generated the data used to validate the server's software, cmuts.

Science 2026 · Cover

Testing AI-designed RNAs at the bench

Generative models design RNAs that have no natural relatives to check against. For a c-di-GMP riboswitch target, FAST-MaP measured whether the starting sequence, the top Eterna design and an AI design (MPNN-fixbb) change shape when the ligand binds.

Ongoing

Designing mRNAs that use structure to control protein output

I engineer structure inside mRNAs to turn protein output down. A matched control shows the effect is sequence-specific, and the translation-efficiency models I tested miss it. On Eterna, mRNA Switch Controls opens this design problem to players.

Preprint

mRNA deadenylases keep cell division robust under microtubule stress

In my PhD, a genome-wide yeast screen showed the PAN2–PAN3 deadenylase is needed for mitosis under microtubule stress. It is the enzyme's activity that matters, and the role holds from yeast to human cells.
