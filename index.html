About

I'm a postdoc with Rhiju Das at Stanford. I invented FAST-MaP to read RNA structure quickly, use it to check whether AI-designed RNAs fold as predicted, and design mRNAs that use structure to control protein output. The results go back to the models, so they can design the medicines of the future.

I came to RNA through cell division, studying chromosome segregation for my master's at JNCASR with Kaustuv Sanyal, and mRNA stability during mitosis for my PhD at the University of British Columbia with Christopher Loewen and Calvin Roskelley.

Research

Preprint

FAST-MaP: chemical mapping of RNA structures without the infrastructure

DNA template to per-nucleotide RNA structure data in under a week, using commercial gene synthesis, Nanopore sequencing and a public analysis server. No sequencing core or custom code needed. I also generated the data used to validate the server's software, cmuts.

Science 2026 · Cover

Checking if AI-designed RNAs actually fold as predicted

FAST-MaP data showed which AI-designed RNA pseudoknots folded as predicted, and guided the next rounds of design.

Ongoing

Designing mRNAs that use structure to control protein output

I engineer structure inside mRNAs to turn protein output down, and a matched control shows the effect is sequence-specific. None of the translation-efficiency models I tested predict it. On Eterna, mRNA Switch Controls opens this design problem to players, and the selected designs are now being tested.

Preprint

mRNA deadenylases keep cell division robust under microtubule stress

In my PhD, a genome-wide yeast screen showed the PAN2–PAN3 deadenylase is needed for mitosis under microtubule stress. It is the enzyme's activity that matters, and the role holds from yeast to human cells.
